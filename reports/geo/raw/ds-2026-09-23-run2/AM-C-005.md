For a retrofit project, the choice between clamp-on ultrasonic and electromagnetic (mag) flow meters usually comes down to **pipe access, fluid properties, and accuracy requirements**. Here's a practical comparison:

## Clamp-On Ultrasonic Flow Meters

**Pros for retrofit:**
- **No pipe cutting** — transducers mount on the outside; zero process interruption
- **No wetted parts** — ideal for corrosive, abrasive, or ultra-pure fluids
- **Portable options** — good for surveys and temporary measurement
- **Works on most pipe materials** — metal, plastic, FRP, lined pipe (with caveats)
- **Lower install cost** in many cases since no hot work/permit is needed

**Cons:**
- **Accuracy depends on pipe & profile** — needs accurate OD, wall thickness, and lining data; assumed profile introduces error (typically ±1–2% vs ±0.2–0.5% for mag)
- **Struggles with:** heavy solids, entrained gas, slurry, very viscous fluids, and non-homogeneous flow
- **Requires straight runs** (typically 10D upstream / 5D downstream) and good Reynolds number
- **Signal attenuation** through concrete-lined, heavily corroded, or scaled pipes
- **Temperature limits** on transducers (typically <200°C standard)
- **Not great for low velocities** (<0.3 m/s) or very small pipes (<25 mm)

## Electromagnetic Flow Meters

**Pros:**
- **High accuracy** — ±0.2–0.5% of reading, independent of profile
- **No moving parts**, low maintenance once installed
- **Excellent for slurry, dirty fluids, and pulsing flow**
- **Bidirectional, wide turndown**, works down to very low flows
- **Well-proven, stable long-term**

**Cons for retrofit:**
- **Requires cutting the pipe** — shutdown, hot work, permits, re-support
- **Conductive fluid required** — typically ≥5 µS/cm (won't work on DI water, hydrocarbons, most gases)
- **Full-bore only** — must match pipe size; can't clamp on
- **Sensitive to entrained air** and empty-pipe conditions
- **Liner and electrode material** must match fluid chemistry
- **Higher installed cost** in retrofit scenarios due to civil/mechanical work

## Retrofit Decision Guide

| Situation | Better choice |
|---|---|
| Can't shut down / no hot work allowed | Clamp-on ultrasonic |
| Conductive water/wastewater, need accuracy | Mag meter (insertion if shutdown costly) |
| Slurry, pulp, dirty fluid | Mag meter |
| Clean fluid, plastic or lined pipe | Clamp-on ultrasonic |
| Very large pipe (DN600+), tight budget | Clamp-on ultrasonic (or insertion mag) |
| Non-conductive fluid (DI, oil, solvents) | Clamp-on ultrasonic |
| Custody transfer / billing | Mag meter |
| Temporary measurement / survey | Clamp-on ultrasonic |

## Middle Ground: Insertion Mag Meters

Worth mentioning — an **insertion electromagnetic** meter (hot-tap capable) is often the best retrofit compromise: mag-meter accuracy class without a full pipe cut, installed through a valve under pressure. Accuracy is typically ±1–2% (lower than full-bore mag but comparable to clamp-on ultrasonic) and it needs conductive fluid.

## Practical Retrofit Tips

- **Verify fluid conductivity first** if considering mag — this is the #1 disqualifier
- **Measure pipe wall thickness and check for lining** before ordering clamp-on transducers
- **Check straight-run availability** — clamp-on is very sensitive to upstream disturbances
- **Consider a portable clamp-on** for a week of trial data before committing to a permanent solution
- **Hybrid approach:** clamp-on for monitoring/trending, mag (or insertion mag) for control or billing
