Chinese instrument manufacturers (covering musical instruments, test & measurement instruments, medical devices, and industrial instrumentation) generally follow a fairly consistent set of payment terms shaped by export norms, order size, and relationship history. Here's the practical breakdown:

## Most Common Terms

**30% T/T deposit + 70% before shipment**
- The default for most small-to-mid-sized orders
- Deposit covers raw materials; balance paid once goods are ready, before the B/L or telex release is issued
- Very common for musical instruments, meters, gauges, and OEM/ODM work

**100% T/T in advance**
- Used for small orders (often under USD 3,000–5,000), samples, or first-time buyers
- Also common when the buyer has no credit history with the supplier

**30% deposit + 70% against B/L copy**
- Middle-ground option for repeat customers or larger orders
- Buyer sees the shipping documents before releasing the balance
- Requires a degree of trust on both sides

## For Larger or Repeat Orders

**Letter of Credit (L/C) at sight**
- Typically for orders above ~USD 50,000–100,000
- Irrevocable L/C confirmed by a prime bank
- Common in industrial instrumentation and medical device exports
- Chinese suppliers often prefer L/Cs issued by Bank of China, HSBC, or similarly rated banks

**30/60/10 or milestone payments**
- For custom or large project work: 30% deposit, 60% before shipment, 10% after installation/acceptance
- More typical for industrial/process instrumentation than for consumer musical instruments

**Open account / T/T 30–60 days after shipment**
- Only offered to long-term, high-volume buyers
- Requires established trust — rare for new relationships

## Other Instruments

- **Western Union / PayPal**: small samples, prototype fees
- **Alibaba Trade Assurance**: acts as escrow; common for first transactions via Alibaba
- **CAD (Cash Against Documents)**: occasionally used, but Chinese suppliers usually prefer payment before releasing originals
- **D/P (Documents against Payment)**: sometimes accepted for mid-size orders, but riskier for the seller

## Practical Notes

- **Currency**: usually USD; RMB increasingly accepted, especially for Asian buyers
- **Platform fees**: PayPal and credit cards add 3–4%; T/T bank charges are typically split or borne by the buyer
- **Negotiability**: everything except 100% advance is negotiable. First orders usually require a deposit; after 2–3 successful transactions, terms loosen
- **Red flags**: a supplier demanding 100% advance on a large first order, or one willing to accept unusually loose terms, both warrant caution
