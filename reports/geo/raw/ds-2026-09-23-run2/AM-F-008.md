Query: how do I get a sample order from a sensor manufacturer

Getting a sample order from a sensor manufacturer follows a fairly standard process. Here's how to approach it:

## 1. Identify the Right Manufacturers
- **Search directories**: Sensors Expo exhibitor lists, ThomasNet, GlobalSpec, Alibaba, Digi-Key, Mouser, RS Components
- **Check distributor vs. manufacturer**: Distributors (Digi-Key, Mouser) sell singles/low quantities easily; going direct to a manufacturer often requires a formal sample request
- **Match your needs**: Consider specs (range, accuracy, interface), certifications, and form factor before reaching out

## 2. Prepare Your Request
Manufacturers filter sample requests by legitimacy, so include:
- **Company info**: Name, website, industry, and your role
- **Application**: What you're building and why this sensor fits
- **Volume potential**: Estimated annual usage if the design goes to production
- **Timeline**: When you need it and your project stage (prototype, eval, etc.)
- **Shipping details**: Address, courier account (many manufacturers ask you to cover freight)

## 3. Contact Channels (in order of effectiveness)
1. **Distributor sites** – easiest; buy 1–5 units online with no approval needed
2. **Manufacturer website sample form** – most have "Request a Sample" or "Evaluate Our Sensor" pages
3. **Sales rep / regional distributor** – email or call; reps can often push samples through faster
4. **Trade shows** – booths frequently hand out samples or set them up on the spot
5. **LinkedIn** – message a field applications engineer (FAE) or regional sales manager

## 4. Typical Outcomes
- **Free samples**: Common for low-cost sensors (<$50) if you show real design intent
- **Paid samples**: Standard for expensive/industrial sensors; often credited back on first production order
- **NDA required**: For custom or high-end sensors before sharing specs
- **Lead time**: 1–3 weeks typical; expedite if you pay freight

## 5. Tips That Improve Your Odds
- Reference a specific part number (not "your temperature sensor")
- Mention competitors you're evaluating — it creates urgency
- Ask for an **evaluation kit** rather than a bare sensor; often easier to approve
- Follow up after 3–4 business days if no response
- Offer to pay — it signals seriousness and usually speeds things up

## 6. After You Receive It
- Confirm receipt and share initial feedback — builds the relationship
- Ask about volume pricing and lead times early
- Get a second contact (FAE) so you're not dependent on one rep
