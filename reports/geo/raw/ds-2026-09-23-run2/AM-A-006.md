Level sensors for chemical reactor tanks are critical instruments that must handle aggressive chemicals, varying temperatures, pressures, and often corrosive or viscous media. Here's a comprehensive overview:

## Key Selection Factors

1. **Chemical compatibility** – wetted materials (PTFE, PFA, Hastelloy, titanium, 316L SS)
2. **Temperature & pressure** – reactors often run 150–300°C and up to 100+ bar
3. **Vessel agitation** – baffles, impellers, foam, and vortices disrupt readings
4. **Process conditions** – vacuum, crystallization, foaming, condensation
5. **Hygiene/safety** – ATEX/IECEx, FDA, EHEDG, SIL ratings
6. **Interface vs. total level** – do you need point detection or continuous measurement?

## Common Technologies

### Continuous Level
| Technology | Best For | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| **Guided Wave Radar (TDR)** | Most chemical reactors; ignores foam/vapor | Coating on probe, needs dielectric >1.4 |
| **Non-Contact Radar (80 GHz)** | Corrosive, hygienic, no contact | Foam, condensation on antenna |
| **Differential Pressure (DP)** | High pressure/temp reactors | Density changes affect accuracy |
| **Magnetostrictive** | Clean liquids, high accuracy | Not for slurries/coating media |
| **Hydrostatic** | Simple, low-cost | Density-dependent |
| **Capacitance/RF** | Point or continuous, cheap | Drift with coating/build-up |
| **Ultrasonic** | Non-contact, cheap | Foam, vapor, temperature gradients |

### Point Level
- **Vibrating fork** – most popular for high/low alarms; works with foam and buildup
- **Capacitance probes**
- **Float switches** – limited chemical compatibility
- **Conductivity probes** – conductive liquids only
- **Optical** – clean liquids

## Typical Recommendation by Application

- **Glass-lined / PTFE-lined reactors** → Non-contact 80 GHz radar or guided radar with PTFE-covered probe
- **High-pressure hydrogenation reactors** → Guided radar or DP with diaphragm seals
- **Polymerization reactors** (viscous, fouling) → Vibrating fork for point; radar with purge for continuous
- **Pharma reactors (CIP/SIP)** → Hygienic 3-A/EHEDG radar or magnetostrictive with sanitary flange
- **Foaming reactors** → Guided radar or DP; ultrasonic is unreliable

## Installation Tips

- **Nozzle placement** away from agitator shaft and inlet stream
- Use **stillwells/bridles** to damp turbulence for float/DP sensors
- For radar, avoid nozzles taller than antenna length
- **Purge/cleaning connections** for fouling media
- Redundant point switches for SIL 2/3 overfill protection
- Grounding is critical for capacitance and RF admittance
