# AM-F-009 — faq / flow

Query: can an electromagnetic flow meter measure non-conductive oil

Engine: deepseek-chat (no web browsing) (model: deepseek-flash)

---

**No, a standard electromagnetic flow meter cannot measure non-conductive oil.**

Here's why:

## How Electromagnetic Flow Meters Work

EM flow meters operate on **Faraday's Law of Induction**: a conductive fluid moving through a magnetic field generates a voltage proportional to its flow velocity. The key requirement is that the fluid must be **electrically conductive** — typically at least **5 µS/cm** (some manufacturers require 10–20 µS/cm for reliable readings).

## Why Oil Fails This Requirement

- **Hydrocarbon oils** (crude, lubricating, hydraulic, diesel, etc.) have conductivities typically in the range of **10⁻¹² to 10⁻⁸ S/m** (roughly 0.000001 to 0.01 µS/cm).
- This is **millions of times less conductive** than the minimum threshold.
- With no meaningful conductivity, no measurable voltage is induced, so the meter reads zero or produces erratic/meaningless output.

## What to Use Instead for Oil

| Meter Type | Suitable for Oil? |
|---|---|
| Positive displacement (PD) | ✅ Yes |
| Turbine | ✅ Yes |
| Coriolis (mass flow) | ✅ Yes |
| Ultrasonic (transit-time or Doppler) | ✅ Yes (clean oils) |
| Vortex | ✅ Yes (low-viscosity oils) |
| **Electromagnetic** | ❌ No |

## Edge Cases / Exceptions

1. **Water-in-oil emulsions** — If an oil stream contains enough entrained water to push conductivity above the threshold, an EM meter *may* work, but readings can be unreliable and non-linear.
2. **Conductive additives** — Some specialty fluids (e.g., certain water-glycol hydraulic fluids) are conductive enough for EM meters, but these aren't really "oils" in the conventional sense.
3. **Two-phase flow** — EM meters handle oil/water mixtures poorly due to slip and non-uniform conductivity.

**Bottom line:** For essentially any real-world oil application, choose a positive displacement, Coriolis, turbine, or ultrasonic meter instead of an electromagnetic one.
