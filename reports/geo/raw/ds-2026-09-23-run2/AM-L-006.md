Here is a comprehensive guide and template for ordering **custom wooden case packaging for a large flow meter**. 

Because large flow meters are high-value, precision instruments, the packaging must protect against impact, vibration, moisture, and movement during transit. 

---

### 1. Information You Need to Gather Before Requesting a Quote
To get an accurate quote from a packaging manufacturer, you will need the following specifications:

**A. Flow Meter Specifications:**

-   **Dimensions:** Length × Width × Height (including any flanges or protruding electronics).
-   **Weight:** Net weight of the meter + any accessories (cables, manuals, spare parts).
-   **Center of Gravity:** Where is the heaviest point? (Usually the main body).
-   **Fragility:** Are there glass displays, delicate sensors, or exposed wiring?
-   **Mounting:** Does it have feet, flanges, or is it a spool piece?

**B. Shipping & Logistics:**

-   **Mode of Transport:** LTL freight, FTL truck, Ocean freight, or Air freight?
-   **Stackability:** Will these cases be stacked? (If yes, the case needs a higher load rating).
-   **Climate:** Will it be exposed to rain, high humidity, or extreme temperatures?
-   **Handling:** Will a forklift be used? (Need forklift pockets).

---

### 2. Recommended Wooden Case Design for Large Flow Meters
For a large flow meter, a standard nailed plywood box is not enough. You should specify a **Heavy-Duty Export Crate**.

-   **Base:** Heavy-duty timber skid (4-way forklift entry). The base must be thick enough to support the weight without flexing.
-   **Walls:** Plywood (minimum 12mm or 15mm depending on weight) or solid wood boards.
-   **Frame:** Internal lumber framing (2x4s or 2x6s) to create a rigid structure.
-   **Lining:** **Vapor barrier (VCI) lining.** This is critical. The inside should be lined with a moisture-barrier film to prevent rust and condensation during ocean transit.
-   **Closure:** Screwed or bolted shut (not just nailed) for easy inspection and secure closure.

---

### 3. Internal Securing (The Most Important Part)
The flow meter cannot move inside the box. You must specify the internal blocking and bracing method:

-   **Option A: Custom Foam Cradles (Best for precision):** 
    -   The meter sits in custom-cut polyethylene (PE) or Ethafoam cradles. 
    -   The foam is glued to the wooden base.
    -   Straps go over the meter and bolt into the wooden base.
-   **Option B: Wooden Blocking & Bracing:**
    -   Wooden blocks are screwed into the base around the flanges or body of the meter to prevent lateral movement.
    -   Threaded rods or heavy-duty ratchet straps secure the meter to the base.
-   **Option C: Suspension (For extreme shock absorption):**
    -   The meter is suspended in a frame with springs or heavy-duty elastic cords. (Rare for very large meters, but used for highly sensitive electronics).

---

### 4. Accessories & Documentation
Large flow meters often come with separate components:

-   **Transmitter/Electronics:** If separate, these should be packed in a separate, smaller foam-lined box inside the crate or shipped in a separate case.
-   **Cables & Bolts:** Pack in a clearly labeled accessory box secured to the inside wall of the crate.
-   **Documentation:** 
    -   Place a waterproof envelope containing the packing list, calibration certificate, and manual inside the crate.
    -   **Critical:** Attach a "Unpacking Instructions" document to the outside of the crate.

---

### 5. Compliance & Markings
Depending on where you are shipping, the crate must comply with regulations:

-   **ISPM 15:** If shipping internationally, all solid wood must be heat-treated or fumigated and stamped with the IPPC logo. (Plywood is exempt, but the skids/framing are not).
-   **Shipping Marks:** 
    -   Consignee name and address.
    -   PO Number / Order Number.
    -   Case Number (e.g., 1 of 3).
    -   Gross Weight / Net Weight.
    -   Dimensions.
    -   **Handling Symbols:** "This Side Up," "Fragile," "Keep Dry," "Center of Gravity" (if off-center).
-   **Shock/Tilt Indicators:** Highly recommended. Attach a "ShockWatch" or "TiltWatch" label to the outside. If the crate is dropped or tipped, the indicator turns red, alerting you to potential damage.

### 7. Pro-Tips for Success
1.  **Ask for a 3D Drawing:** Before they build it, ask the packaging vendor for a 3D CAD drawing or a detailed schematic. Verify that the internal supports match your meter's exact geometry.
2.  **Photo Documentation:** Take photos of the meter before it is packed, and photos of the meter inside the crate before it is sealed. This is essential for insurance claims if damage occurs.
3.  **Test the Prototype:** If this is a recurring order, pay for one prototype case. Ship it to yourself (or do a drop test) to ensure the design works before ordering 50 of them.
4.  **Weight Limits:** Ensure the skid is rated for the weight. A 1,000 lb flow meter needs a skid designed for at least 2,000 lbs to account for dynamic loads during trucking.
