Typical payment and shipping terms when sourcing instruments from Chinese suppliers depend on the product category (musical instruments, lab/medical instruments, industrial meters, etc.), order size, and whether you buy through a trading company or directly from a factory. Here’s a practical overview of what’s standard and what to negotiate.

## Payment Terms

**Most common for new buyers**
- **30% T/T deposit + 70% balance before shipment** — the default for most Chinese suppliers, especially for custom or OEM orders.
- **100% T/T in advance** — common for small orders (samples, low-value), but risky for buyers. Avoid for anything substantial unless the supplier is verified.
- **Alibaba Trade Assurance / escrow** — safest for first orders; payment released only after you confirm delivery per contract. Many suppliers accept this if pushed.

**For established relationships**
- **30% deposit + 70% against B/L copy** — buyer gets some protection via the bill of lading.
- **L/C at sight (Letter of Credit)** — used for larger orders ($50k+); adds bank fees but balances risk.
- **O/A (Open Account), 30–60 days** — rare with Chinese suppliers unless you’re a large, long-term buyer.
- **D/P (Documents against Payment)** — occasionally accepted; safer than O/A for the buyer.

**Payment methods**
- **T/T (bank wire)** — standard. Confirm the beneficiary name matches the contract exactly; watch for BEC (email hacking) fraud.
- **Alipay / WeChat Pay** — small domestic-style payments, not ideal for international B2B.
- **PayPal** — some suppliers accept for samples, but fees are high (4–5%) and often passed to you.
- **Western Union** — avoid; no recourse, common in scams.
- **Credit card via Trade Assurance** — possible, adds ~3% fee.

**Key protections to insist on**
- Payment to a **company account matching the contract/company name** (not a personal account).
- Specify **currency** (USD, EUR, RMB) and **who bears bank charges** (usually each side pays their own).
- Tie final payment to **inspection** (pre-shipment inspection, e.g. SGS, BV, or your own QC).

## Shipping Terms (Incoterms 2020)

**Common choices for Chinese exports**
- **FOB (Free On Board)** — most common. Supplier delivers to port (e.g., Shanghai, Shenzhen, Ningbo); you control freight and insurance. Best if you have a freight forwarder.
- **EXW (Ex Works)** — you pick up from the factory; cheapest quote but you handle all export logistics. Only practical if you have a China-based agent.
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)** — supplier arranges shipping to your port. Convenient but you lose control and may overpay.
- **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)** — supplier handles everything to your door, including duties. Easiest, most expensive, and sometimes problematic for customs compliance.
- **DAP (Delivered at Place)** — like DDP but buyer pays import duties/taxes. Increasingly popular.

**Shipping modes**
- **Sea freight (LCL/FCL)** — for bulky/heavy instruments (pianos, drums, lab equipment). 25–40 days transit; LCL for <15 CBM, FCL for larger.
- **Air freight** — for high-value, small/medium items (violins, meters, sensors). 5–10 days; expensive per kg.
- **Express (DHL/FedEx/UPS)** — samples and small orders, 3–7 days, door-to-door.
- **Rail (China–Europe)** — 15–25 days, good middle option for EU buyers.

**Packaging & insurance**
- Confirm **export packaging** (wooden crates for fragile instruments; ISPM 15 heat-treated wood for crates to avoid customs issues).
- Buy **marine/air cargo insurance** — usually 0.1–0.3% of cargo value; don’t rely on carrier liability.
- For instruments, insist on **shock/moisture protection** and humidity control (especially for acoustic instruments).

## Practical Tips

- **Start small**: sample order first, then scale.
- **Use a contract** with specs, tolerances, lead times, penalties for delay, and inspection terms.
- **Pre-shipment inspection** is cheap insurance on orders over ~$5k.
- **Verify the supplier**: business license, export license, factory audit (Alibaba Gold Supplier is a weak signal).
- **Watch lead times**: 15–45 days production is normal; add 10–20 days buffer around Chinese New Year (Jan/Feb) when factories shut for 2–4 weeks.
- **HS codes & duties**: confirm the correct HS code for your instrument; wrong codes cause delays and unexpected duties.
