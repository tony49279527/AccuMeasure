Clamp-on (non-invasive) ultrasonic flow meters are an excellent choice for measuring flow in existing water pipes because they install without cutting the pipe or interrupting service. Here's a practical overview.

## How They Work

A clamp-on meter straps transducers onto the **outside** of the pipe. It measures flow using one of two principles:

- **Transit-time (time-of-flight):** Most common for clean water. Measures the difference in time for an ultrasonic pulse to travel with vs. against the flow. Accurate and cost-effective.
- **Doppler:** Used for fluids with bubbles or solids (e.g., wastewater, slurries). Less common for clean municipal water.

For typical water pipes, **transit-time is the right choice.**

## Key Selection Criteria

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Pipe material** | Must be "sonically conductive" — metal, PVC, HDPE, copper all work. Concrete/lined pipes need special handling. |
| **Pipe size (OD & wall)** | Determines transducer spacing and whether the meter can achieve good signal. |
| **Liner/coating** | Cement-lined ductile iron or epoxy liners can block or distort the signal. |
| **Fluid** | Clean water = transit-time. Entrained air or particles = Doppler or hybrid. |
| **Temperature & pressure** | Affects transducer choice and coupling. |
| **Flow range** | Check turndown ratio and low-flow cutoff vs. your actual range. |
| **Accuracy needed** | Typically ±1–2% of reading; some high-end models claim ±0.5%. |
| **Straight pipe run** | Usually 10×D upstream, 5×D downstream (more if bends/valves nearby). |
| **Outputs** | 4–20 mA, pulse, Modbus/HART, datalogging. |

## Advantages

- **No shutdown, no pipe cutting** — install on live pipes
- **No pressure loss** — nothing protrudes into the flow
- **Portable or fixed** options
- **Retrofit-friendly** for existing infrastructure
- Works on many pipe materials and sizes (typically ½" to 200"+)

## Limitations & Watch-Outs

- **Accuracy depends heavily on installation** — transducer spacing, coupling, and pipe condition matter more than the meter's spec sheet
- **Pipe wall condition** — heavy scaling, pitting, or rough interior degrades signal
- **Liners and coatings** — cement-lined pipes are a common failure point
- **Entrained air** — bubbles from pumps or aeration wreck transit-time readings
- **Low-flow performance** — clamp-on meters struggle below ~0.3 m/s
- **Conductive pipe ≠ conductive fluid** — the *pipe* can be metal, but the *fluid* needs to transmit ultrasound (water is fine)
- **Cost** — higher than inline mag meters for the equivalent accuracy in large sizes, but no install cost

## Common Brands

- **Fixed/industrial:** Fuji Electric (FSZ), Siemens (SITRANS FS), Endress+Hauser (Proline Prosonic Flow), KROHNE (OPTISONIC), GE/Panametrics (PT878, AT600)
- **Portable/field:** Fuji Portaflow, GE PT878, Flexim (F601, F721 — very popular for water audits)
- **Budget/industrial:** Dynasonics (Badger), Greyline, Micronics

**Flexim** and **Fuji** are especially well-regarded for water utility work.

## Practical Tips

1. **Do a site survey first** — measure OD, wall thickness, and check for liner. A phone call to the manufacturer's app engineer with these numbers will confirm feasibility.
2. **Pick a good location** — long straight run, no air pockets, no valves/pumps immediately upstream.
3. **Use proper coupling compound** — not optional; dry coupling kills accuracy.
4. **Verify against a known reference** — e.g., a portable meter or pump curve — before committing.
5. **Consider a portable unit for diagnostics** before buying a fixed one. A week of portable logging often pays for itself in avoided mistakes.
6. **Watch the Reynolds number range** — very low flows in large pipes are the hardest case.
