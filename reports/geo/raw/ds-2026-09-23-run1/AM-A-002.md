Ultrasonic level sensors are a popular choice for water treatment tanks because they provide **non-contact measurement**, which minimizes maintenance, clogging, and contamination issues common in dirty or chemically treated water.

Here's a practical breakdown for selecting and applying them:

## How They Work
A transducer emits an ultrasonic pulse downward, measures the time for the echo to return from the water surface, and calculates distance. Level = tank height − distance to surface (accounting for the sensor's dead zone).

## Why They Fit Water Treatment
- **Non-contact** – no fouling from sludge, grease, or scaling
- **No moving parts** – low maintenance
- **Works with corrosive/chemical liquids** – wetted materials only matter for the transducer face
- **Easy retrofit** – mounts on top of existing tanks
- **4–20 mA, HART, Modbus, or relay outputs** – integrates with SCADA/PLC

## Key Specs to Match to Your Application

| Parameter | What to Check |
|---|---|
| **Range** | Typically 0.25–15 m; pick based on tank depth + dead zone |
| **Dead zone (blanking)** | Usually 0.25–0.5 m; sensor must sit above max level |
| **Beam angle** | 5°–12° typical; narrower is better for tall/narrow tanks |
| **Accuracy** | ±0.25% of range typical |
| **Frequency** | 40–80 kHz; higher freq = narrower beam, shorter range |
| **Output** | 4–20 mA, HART, RS-485/Modbus, relays |
| **Ingress protection** | IP67/IP68 for outdoor or washdown |
| **Materials** | PVDF, PP, or PTFE face for chemicals; PVC for potable water |
| **Temperature** | Many have built-in temp compensation |

## Where They Work Well
- **Clear/filtered water tanks** – excellent accuracy
- **Chemical dosing tanks** (hypochlorite, alum, polymer) – with correct wetted material
- **Potable water storage** – food-grade materials
- **Equalization and aeration basins** – non-contact avoids fouling

## Where They Struggle
- **Foam on surface** – absorbs/scatters the pulse
- **Heavy vapor, mist, or condensation** – false echoes
- **Turbulent or splashing surfaces** – erratic readings
- **Steam or temperature stratification**
- **Very narrow tanks** – beam reflects off walls
- **Vacuum or pressurized tanks** – need specialized models

For these cases, consider **guided wave radar (GWR)**, **radar (80 GHz)**, **hydrostatic pressure**, or **float/conductive** sensors.

## Installation Tips
1. Mount **perpendicular** to water surface.
2. Keep the beam clear of pipes, ladders, and walls (use beam angle math).
3. Maintain clearance above the **dead zone** at max fill.
4. Use a **stilling well** in turbulent tanks.
5. Add **sun shield** for outdoor installations.
6. Ground the tank if it's metal.
7. For potable water, verify NSF/ANSI 61 compliance.
