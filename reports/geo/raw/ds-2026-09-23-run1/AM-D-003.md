## Selection Criteria for Water Utility Projects

1. **Certifications** – NSF/ANSI 61 & 372 (potable water, USA), WRAS (UK), ACS (France), KTW (Germany).
2. **Accuracy class** – ±0.2% or ±0.5% for billing; ±1% acceptable for internal monitoring.
3. **Line size** – DN15–DN3000; full-bore vs. insertion type depending on pipe size and budget.
4. **Liner & electrode material** – EPDM liner + stainless steel/Hastelloy electrodes for potable water.
5. **Communication** – 4–20 mA, HART, Modbus, pulse output, or NB-IoT/LoRaWAN for AMI.
6. **IP rating** – IP68 for buried or flooded chambers (common in DMA/district metering).
7. **Battery-powered options** – For remote district metering where mains power is unavailable (e.g., ABB AquaMaster, KROHNE WATERFLUX 3070).
8. **Local support & calibration** – Critical for large utility frameworks; prefer regional service centers.

## Typical Recommendations by Application

- **Bulk transmission / treatment plant:** Endress+Hauser Promag W, KROHNE OPTIFLUX 4000
- **District metered areas (DMA):** ABB AquaMaster, KROHNE WATERFLUX 3070
- **Billing / custody transfer:** Endress+Hauser Promag L, Siemens MAG 6000
- **Budget large-scale rollout:** Supmea, Badger M-Series, Xylem iPERL

## Practical Advice

- For **public tenders**, Tier 1 brands usually win on spec compliance.
- For **private utilities or cost-sensitive projects**, Supmea/Sinomeasure with NSF or WRAS certification can be viable if local service is available.
- Always request **factory calibration certificates** traceable to national standards.
- Verify **lead times** — Tier 1 mag meters can run 6–12 weeks; Asian suppliers often 2–4 weeks.
