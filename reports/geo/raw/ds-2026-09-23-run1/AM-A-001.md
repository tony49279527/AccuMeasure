Radar level sensors are the current industry standard for crude oil storage tank gauging, largely replacing float-based and servo mechanical systems in new installations. Here's a practical overview:

## Why Radar for Crude Oil?

- **Non-contact** (or minimally invasive) measurement — no moving parts to foul on wax, paraffin, or sludge
- **Accuracy** typically ±1–3 mm for custody transfer applications
- **Works under vapor, foam, and changing density** — critical since crude density varies with temperature and composition
- **Suitable for API 2350 / OISD / IEC 61511** overfill protection when SIL-rated

## Two Main Technologies

| Type | Frequency | Best For | Notes |
|------|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Pulse / FMCW** | 6–26 GHz (K, X band) | General crude service | Lower cost, narrower beam at higher freq |
| **FMCW 80 GHz** | 76–81 GHz | Tanks with internals, foam, short nozzles | Very narrow beam (~3–4°), best for stilling wells and obstructions |
| **Guided Wave Radar (GWR)** | ~1 GHz TDR | Clean hydrocarbons, interface measurement | Contact type; probe can coat with paraffin |

For crude, **80 GHz non-contact FMCW** is now the most common choice — narrow beam avoids reflections from tank walls, mixers, and heating coils.

## Key Selection Criteria

1. **Tank type** — fixed roof, floating roof, internal floating roof (IFR). Floating roofs need radar aimed at the roof deck, not the liquid.
2. **Antenna** — horn, lens, or parabolic. Lens antennas resist condensation and crude buildup better.
3. **Nozzle length & diameter** — long nozzles require antenna extension or 80 GHz to avoid multiple reflections.
4. **Process conditions** — pressure (atmospheric vs. blanketed), temperature, H₂S, vapor composition.
5. **Accuracy class** — inventory (±10 mm) vs. custody transfer (±1–3 mm, requires certified proving).
6. **Safety** — ATEX/IECEx Zone 0/1, SIL 2/3 for overfill prevention.

## Common Products

- **Emerson Rosemount 5900S / 5408** — custody transfer, API-compliant
- **Endress+Hauser Micropilot S FMR540 / FMR5xx** — SIL-rated, IFR-capable
- **VEGA VEGAPULS 6X (80 GHz)** — versatile, good for IFR and foam
- **Honeywell Enraf 9700 / 990** — legacy Enraf systems widely used in tank farms
- **Krohne OPTIWAVE 7500 C** — 80 GHz, custody transfer

## Installation Best Practices for Crude Tanks

- Mount in a **stilling well** for agitated or floating-roof tanks to eliminate surface movement errors
- Keep beam clear of **mixers, heating coils, inlet diffusers, and support columns**
- Use **drip-off / purged antenna** in tanks with heavy wax or condensation
- For **custody transfer**, combine radar with **multiple temperature sensors (RTDs on a multipoint)** and pressure for net standard volume (NSV) per API MPMS Ch. 12.1
- Pair with **Tank Gauging System (TGS)** for inventory, leak detection, and overfill (API 2350)

## Limitations to Watch

- **Foam** (from high pour-point or asphaltenic crude) can absorb or scatter the signal — 80 GHz handles this better but not perfectly
- **Heavy vapor / mist** attenuates higher frequencies slightly
- **Condensation** on the antenna face biases readings — use lens antennas or heated versions
- **Floating roof tilt / sticking** — if the roof isn't freely floating, level reading is misleading; use a separate roof-level sensor
