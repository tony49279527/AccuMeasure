# 80 GHz vs 26 GHz Radar Level Transmitters for Storage Tanks

## Quick Summary

| Aspect | 26 GHz | 80 GHz |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Beam angle | ~10° | ~3–6° |
| Antenna size | 40–80 mm typical | 20–40 mm typical |
| Resolution | Good | Better |
| Foam/condensation immunity | Moderate | Better |
| Price | Lower | Higher |
| Best for | Clean, large tanks | Nozzles, foam, agitation, small tanks |

---

## 1. Beam Angle & Installation

**26 GHz** — Beam angle ~10°. Requires larger antennas and longer unobstructed nozzles. Risk of reflections from tank walls, agitators, or nozzles when mounted close to obstructions.

**80 GHz** — Beam angle ~3–6°. Much tighter beam means:
- Smaller antennas (can fit DN50 or smaller nozzles)
- Easier to avoid internal obstructions
- Better for tall, narrow tanks or tanks with mixers/baffles
- Can mount closer to the wall

## 2. Accuracy & Resolution

- **26 GHz**: ±3–10 mm typical
- **80 GHz**: ±1–2 mm typical, with wider bandwidth giving better signal processing and closer minimum measuring distance (often 50–100 mm from antenna)

For custody transfer or tight inventory control, 80 GHz is preferred.

## 3. Process Conditions

| Condition | 26 GHz | 80 GHz |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Condensation | Fair | Good (self-cleaning lens) |
| Foam | Fair | Better penetration |
| Dust/vapor | Fair | Better |
| Low dielectric (ε < 2) | Marginal | Better |
| Agitated surface | Fair | Better |
| High temp/pressure | Both ok | Both ok |

80 GHz handles **low-dielectric media** (e.g., some solvents, liquefied gases) better because of shorter wavelength and higher dynamic range.

## 4. Storage Tank Applications

**Choose 26 GHz when:**
- Large, clean tanks (e.g., water, fuel oil, bulk chemicals)
- Wide nozzles available
- Budget is the priority
- Existing 26 GHz infrastructure

**Choose 80 GHz when:**
- Small nozzles or restricted mounting
- Tank has agitators, heating coils, or internals
- Product foams or has low dielectric constant
- Requires high accuracy for inventory/custody transfer
- Hygienic or sanitary tanks (smaller antenna = easier cleaning)

## 5. Practical Considerations

- **Retrofit**: 80 GHz often drops into existing 26 GHz nozzles with an adapter — no vessel modification.
- **Vapor/gas layers**: 80 GHz performs better through heavy vapor (e.g., ammonia, LPG).
- **Cost**: 80 GHz units are typically 20–50% more expensive but prices have dropped significantly.
- **Power**: Both are loop-powered 4–20 mA/HART; some 80 GHz offer 2-wire with Bluetooth.

## Bottom Line

For **new installations on storage tanks** — especially with small nozzles, foam, agitation, or accuracy needs — go **80 GHz**. For **large, clean, low-complexity tanks on a tight budget**, **26 GHz** remains a solid, proven choice.
