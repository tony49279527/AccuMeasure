## Title

Portable Flow Meter vs. Fixed Mass Flow Meter: A Procurement Guide

## Subtitle

How industrial buyers, system integrators, importers, and distributors can separate temporary pipe surveys from permanent gas-flow measurement before requesting a quotation.

## Topics

Industrial Automation, Flow Meters, Procurement, Engineering, Manufacturing

## Article Body

Searches for "mass portable flow meters" often combine two separate requirements: a meter that can move from site to site and a meter that reports mass flow for a defined process. Treating those as the same requirement can send an RFQ toward the wrong technology before the supplier has even seen the pipe, fluid, or installation conditions.

For procurement teams, the useful first question is not "Which portable mass flow meter is best?" It is "What decision will this instrument support?" A temporary survey of several installed pipes is a different assignment from permanent measurement of a gas stream in a laboratory, semiconductor line, or process skid. The selection criteria, documents, and acceptance checks should reflect that difference.

AccuMeasure's [flow-meter selection guide](https://www.accumeasuretech.com/products/flow) separates electromagnetic, clamp-on ultrasonic, and thermal mass options by fluid, installation, and duty. That separation is the right starting point for an RFQ: define the service first, then ask a supplier to confirm the proposed configuration against current controlled documents.

## 1. Decide Whether the Job Is a Survey or a Permanent Measurement Point

A portable instrument is usually purchased because the team needs temporary information: a commissioning check, a retrofit survey, a comparison across several lines, or a way to investigate a process without cutting into the pipe. In that case, portability is not a convenience feature alone. It changes the installation method, the transducer arrangement, the pipe data required, and the way the reading will be used.

The published AM-UF200 information describes a clamp-on ultrasonic flow meter with portable and fixed versions. Its sensors mount externally, so the stated use case avoids pipe cutting and process shutdown. The same published guidance describes portable ultrasonic measurement as volumetric flow measurement on prepared pipes, not as a portable thermal-mass instrument.

A permanent mass-flow requirement starts from a different question: which gas, at which operating conditions, is to be measured and controlled? The published AM-MF50 information describes a thermal mass meter for gas measurement in laboratory and semiconductor use. The current flow category page specifically distinguishes that product from a handheld portable mass meter for large process lines. That distinction should be written into the RFQ instead of being left to a catalog search term.

## 2. State the Fluid and Pipe Conditions Before Comparing Technologies

Technology selection depends on the process, not just the word "flow" in the project title. A buyer should identify the fluid or gas, its operating range, the required measurement purpose, and the installed pipe conditions before asking for a model.

For a liquid line, state whether the liquid is conductive, whether the pipe is normally full, and whether the process can be stopped. The published comparison guidance says electromagnetic meters require a conductive liquid, while clamp-on ultrasonic can be a starting point when a pipe cannot be cut or when the fluid is poorly conductive but can transmit ultrasound. Those are preliminary screening rules, not permission to select a meter without a site review.

For a clamp-on survey, include pipe outside diameter, wall thickness, material, liner, orientation, available straight runs, ambient conditions, and physical access around the pipe. A portable clamp-on option still needs a confirmed transducer arrangement and a prepared measurement location. A device that is easy to move is not automatically suitable for every line.

For a gas-flow requirement, specify gas composition, minimum, normal, and maximum flow, pressure, temperature, connection, and the control or reporting role of the measurement. If the requirement is really a field survey, say so. If it is a fixed process measurement, say that instead. This one sentence can prevent a supplier from proposing a portable survey product where a permanent gas meter is needed, or the reverse.

## 3. Define What "Mass" Means for the Project

The term "mass flow" should be connected to an application requirement, not treated as a generic synonym for flow measurement. Procurement teams should clarify whether the process needs a gas mass-flow value for a defined line, a temporary volumetric survey of installed piping, or a comparison between two operating points.

That clarification matters for system integration. Identify whether the result will be read locally, sent to a PLC or other control system, used for trend analysis, or checked only during commissioning. Also state the required output, supply, display, cable, connector, enclosure, and any communication expectation. These requirements belong in the first RFQ, not in a follow-up after a supplier has issued a quotation.

Do not infer a fixed measurement capability from a portable product description, or infer field portability from a mass-flow product description. Ask the supplier to state the measurement principle, applicable fluid phase, installation method, quoted configuration, and any conditions that would make the requested duty unsuitable.

## 4. Send a Procurement-Ready RFQ

A useful RFQ gives the supplier enough information to identify open questions instead of forcing assumptions. At minimum, include:

- Project type: permanent installation, retrofit, temporary audit, commissioning check, skid, or OEM integration.
- Process: liquid or gas, fluid name, conductivity where relevant, solids content, viscosity, temperature, pressure, and minimum, normal, and maximum flow.
- Pipe and site: nominal and actual pipe dimensions, material, liner, connection standard, orientation, straight-run availability, access, weather, washdown, and hazardous-area conditions where applicable.
- Measurement and integration: intended measurement result, performance requirement and error basis, flow direction, output, power, local or remote display, and control-system interface.
- Commercial scope: sample or first-order quantity, target delivery date, destination, trade term, packaging requirements, and approval milestones.

For distributors and importers, add document language, country-specific requirements, and the point at which the end user will approve the technical configuration. For an OEM project, add the product-code rules, label content, packaging artwork, change-notification requirements, and the boundary between standard configuration and non-standard engineering. Do not send confidential drawings or protected artwork until permitted use, ownership, and retention are agreed in writing.

## 5. Request the Documents That Make the Quote Comparable

The strongest procurement comparison is not a price table. It is a document set that makes each supplier's proposal comparable. Before placing an order, request the current datasheet, dimensional drawing, installation instructions, wiring and communication documentation, and the documentation required for the project scope.

Where calibration, inspection, material information, declarations, or hazardous-area documentation are relevant, request the exact document type and the proposed model scope. Ask the supplier to identify revision, issuer where applicable, and how the document will relate to the quoted configuration. A generic statement that a document is "available" does not show whether it applies to the item being purchased.

For a portable survey, agree how the pipe condition, transducer fit, and measurement point will be checked. For a fixed gas-flow application, agree what configuration will be reviewed before shipment and what records are needed for site acceptance. In both cases, the supplier should be able to identify remaining technical questions in writing.

## Final Procurement Rule

Choose the procurement path before choosing the product category. A portable clamp-on survey request should begin with pipe access, pipe construction, fluid behavior, and the temporary decision the measurement must support. A fixed gas mass-flow request should begin with gas conditions, the permanent installation, and the required integration. When an RFQ says only "mass portable flow meter," the correct response is to clarify the duty - not to assume that one instrument class will fit both jobs.
